# Problem 41

Classify the positive integer triples for which the ideal specified below is normal.

## Definitions

Let $k$ be an arbitrary field and let $R=k[x,y,z]$, where $x,y,z$ are independent indeterminates. For an ideal $H\subseteq R$, its integral closure is
$$
\overline H
=\left\{
u\in R:
\begin{array}{l}
\text{there exist }m\ge 1\text{ and }c_i\in H^i\ (1\le i\le m)\\
\text{such that }u^m+c_1u^{m-1}+\cdots+c_m=0
\end{array}
\right\}.
$$
Here $H^i$ is the ideal generated by all products of $i$ elements of $H$. The ideal $H$ is integrally closed if $\overline H=H$, and is normal if every positive integer power of $H$ is integrally closed.

For any $(a,b,c)\in\mathbb Z_{>0}^3$, define
$$
I_{a,b,c}=\overline{(x^a,y^b,z^c)}\subseteq k[x,y,z].
$$

## Task

Give a necessary and sufficient criterion classifying all triples $(a,b,c)\in\mathbb Z_{>0}^3$ for which
$$
\boxed{\ \overline{I_{a,b,c}^{\,n}}=I_{a,b,c}^{\,n}
\quad\text{for every integer }n\ge 1.\ }
$$
The classification and proof must apply over arbitrary fields, with any claimed dependence or independence on the field justified.

## Completion requirements

Prove necessity and sufficiency for all positive triples and all powers. The criterion must determine which triples qualify, rather than restating normality. If an algorithmic criterion is given, prove that it terminates for every triple and that its output is correct. Checking a bounded collection of triples or powers alone does not complete the task. You may use established theorems, but state their precise forms and check their hypotheses.
