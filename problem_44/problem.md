# Problem 44

Construct a two-dimensional complete normal Noetherian local domain in which every ideal primary to the maximal ideal has at least two Rees valuation rings.

## Definitions

All rings are commutative with identity, with $1\ne 0$. A Noetherian local domain $(R,\mathfrak m)$ is an integral domain whose ideals are finitely generated and whose unique maximal ideal is $\mathfrak m$. Its dimension is its Krull dimension, defined by lengths of strict chains of prime ideals. Put $K=\operatorname{Frac}(R)$.

The domain $R$ is normal if every element of $K$ integral over $R$ belongs to $R$. It is complete if the natural map
$$
R\longrightarrow\varprojlim_{n\ge 1}R/\mathfrak m^n
$$
is an isomorphism.

For a proper ideal $I$ of $R$, write
$$
\sqrt I=\{r\in R:r^n\in I\text{ for some }n\ge 1\}.
$$
In this Noetherian local setting, $I$ is $\mathfrak m$-primary if $\sqrt I=\mathfrak m$.

For an ideal $H\subseteq R$, define its integral closure by
$$
\overline H
=\{u\in R:
u^q+c_1u^{q-1}+\cdots+c_q=0
\text{ for some }q\ge 1\text{ and }c_i\in H^i\}.
$$
The power $H^i$ is generated by products of $i$ elements of $H$.

A discrete valuation ring of $K$ is a subring
$$
V=\{0\}\cup\{x\in K^\times:v(x)\ge 0\},
$$
where $v:K^\times\to\mathbb Z$ is surjective, $v(xy)=v(x)+v(y)$, and $v(x+y)\ge\min\{v(x),v(y)\}$ whenever $x+y\ne 0$.

For a nonzero proper ideal $I$ of the Noetherian domain $R$, a set of Rees valuation rings is a finite set $\{V_1,\ldots,V_s\}$ of discrete valuation rings of $K$, each containing $R$, such that
$$
\overline{I^n}
=\bigcap_{i=1}^s (I^nV_i\cap R)
\qquad\text{for every integer }n\ge 1,
$$
and no smaller set of such valuation rings satisfies these identities for all $n\ge 1$. Here $I^nV_i$ is the ideal of $V_i$ generated by $I^n$.

## Task

Give an explicit ring $(R,\mathfrak m)$ and prove that it is a Noetherian local integral domain, complete in its $\mathfrak m$-adic topology, normal, and of Krull dimension exactly two. Prove also that
$$
\boxed{
\text{every }\mathfrak m\text{-primary ideal }I\subseteq R
\text{ has at least two Rees valuation rings}.
}
$$

## Completion requirements

Specify the construction precisely enough to determine the ring and its maximal ideal. Verify every required ring property and prove the assertion for every $\mathfrak m$-primary ideal, not merely a selected family or the maximal ideal itself. Displaying a redundant set of two valuations does not prove that the number of Rees valuation rings is at least two. You may use established theorems, but state their precise forms and check their hypotheses; merely citing the existence of a ring with the requested properties is not a construction.
