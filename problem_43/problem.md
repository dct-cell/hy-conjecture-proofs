# Problem 43

Construct a counterexample to a substantive relaxation of one hypothesis of the generalized Lipman-Sathaye theorem stated below. Choose the hypothesis and its relaxation yourself. The task concerns a weakened statement, not a counterexample to the original theorem.

## Definitions

All rings are commutative with identity. For a Noetherian ring $U$, $\operatorname{Spec}(U)$ is its set of prime ideals, with Zariski closed subsets
$$
V(H)=\{\mathfrak p\in\operatorname{Spec}(U):H\subseteq\mathfrak p\}
$$
for ideals $H$. Dimension and height are Krull dimension and prime-ideal height, defined by lengths of strict chains of prime ideals.

The depth of a nonzero Noetherian local ring $(A,\mathfrak n)$ is the supremum of lengths of regular sequences in $\mathfrak n$. A sequence $a_1,\ldots,a_t\in\mathfrak n$ is regular if multiplication by $a_i$ is injective on $A/(a_1,\ldots,a_{i-1})$ for each $i$.

A Noetherian ring $U$ satisfies Serre's condition $S_2$ if
$$
\operatorname{depth}U_{\mathfrak p}
\ge \min\{2,\dim U_{\mathfrak p}\}
\quad\text{for every }\mathfrak p\in\operatorname{Spec}(U).
$$
Its $S_2$-locus is
$$
\{\mathfrak p\in\operatorname{Spec}(U):
U_{\mathfrak p}\text{ satisfies }S_2\}.
$$
A local Noetherian ring is regular if its maximal ideal can be generated by $\dim A$ elements. A domain is normal if it is integrally closed in its fraction field.

An algebra is essentially of finite type over a ring if it is a localization of a finitely generated algebra over that ring. For a Noetherian domain $A$, an $A$-algebra $B$ has a relatively $S_2$ presentation if there is a surjection
$$
T\longrightarrow B,\qquad
T=W^{-1}A[X_1,\ldots,X_n],
$$
whose kernel $I$ has pure height $h$, meaning that all primes minimal over $I$ have height $h$, and such that
$$
\operatorname{depth}T_{\mathfrak q}
\ge\min\{\operatorname{ht}_T\mathfrak q,h+2\}
\quad\text{for every }\mathfrak q\in\operatorname{Spec}(T)
\text{ containing }I.
$$

For an algebra $U$ essentially of finite type over a Noetherian ring $R$, its Jacobian ideal $J_{U/R}$ is defined as follows. Choose a presentation
$$
U=W^{-1}\bigl(R[X_1,\ldots,X_n]/(f_1,\ldots,f_m)\bigr).
$$
Then $J_{U/R}$ is the ideal of $U$ generated by the images of all $n\times n$ minors of the matrix
$$
\left(\frac{\partial f_i}{\partial X_j}\right)_
{1\le i\le m,\ 1\le j\le n}.
$$
If $m<n$, this ideal is zero; for $n=0$ the empty minor is $1$. This definition is independent of the presentation.

If $S$ contains a domain $R$ with fraction field $K$, then $S$ is torsion-free over $R$ if $rs=0$, $0\ne r\in R$, implies $s=0$. It is generically étale here if
$$
K\otimes_R S
$$
is a finite product of finite separable field extensions of $K$. In the torsion-free case identify $S$ with its image in $L=K\otimes_R S$. Its integral closure $\overline S$ in $L$ consists of the elements of $L$ satisfying a monic polynomial with coefficients in $S$. Module-finite means finitely generated as a module.

For a subring $A\subseteq L$ and an ideal $H$ of $A$, use the colon notation
$$
(A:_L H)=\{z\in L:zH\subseteq A\}.
$$

## Original theorem

Let $R$ be a Noetherian domain with fraction field $K$, and let $S$ be an extension algebra of $R$. Assume all of the following:

1. Every algebra essentially of finite type over $R$ has an open $S_2$-locus.
2. The algebra $S$ is essentially of finite type over $R$, torsion-free over $R$, and generically étale over $R$.
3. For every maximal ideal $M$ of $S$, with $P=M\cap R$, the local domain $R_P$ is normal and $S_M$ has a relatively $S_2$ presentation over $R_P$.
4. With $L=K\otimes_R S$ and $\overline S$ the integral closure of $S$ in $L$, the ring $\overline S$ is module-finite over $S$.
5. For every height-one prime ideal $Q$ of $\overline S$, the local ring $R_{Q\cap R}$ is regular.

Then
$$
\boxed{
(\overline S:_L J_{\overline S/R})
\subseteq
(S:_L J_{S/R}).
}
$$

## Task and completion requirements

Select one substantive hypothesis of this theorem and state a precise, strictly weaker replacement, keeping the other hypotheses unchanged. Deleting the chosen hypothesis is also allowed. Explain why the original hypothesis implies the replacement.

Give explicit rings and structure maps satisfying every retained hypothesis and the replacement, but not the selected original hypothesis. All objects in the displayed conclusion must remain defined. Prove that the conclusion fails by exhibiting
$$
z\in(\overline S:_L J_{\overline S/R})
\setminus(S:_L J_{S/R}).
$$
Verify all ring properties and ideal computations used in the counterexample. The replacement must be a mathematical weakening of an assumption, not a condition formulated merely as the failure of the desired conclusion. One fully verified relaxation and counterexample completes the task; a classification of all possible relaxations is not required.

You may use established theorems, but state their precise forms and check their hypotheses.
