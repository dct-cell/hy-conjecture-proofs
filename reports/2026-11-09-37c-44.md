# Problems 37c and 44: partial formalization audit

**Neither complete problem has passed formal verification.** The repository
continues to contain three complete formalizations: 5a, 32b and 41. This report
records newly verified local developments and identifies their exact limits.
Problem 43's previously reported partial development is unchanged.

## Problem 37c

The local project separates the full `Problem37C.Counterexample` specification
from the partial `DigitCoreCertificate`. No value of the former is constructed.
The full specification retains a finite-type ambient algebra, an intermediate
subalgebra and a prime localization that fails the usual Jaffard definition.
Using a field as the Noetherian base is a valid specialization for this
counterexample.

The verified development includes the two quadratic arrows and coordinate
changes; inverse polynomial-algebra isomorphisms after inverting the parameter;
the contraction estimate; existence and uniqueness of the complete infinite
power-series orbit; and persistence of a nonzero polynomial value after fixing
a sufficiently long digit prefix. The orbit theorems quantify over arbitrary
infinite sequences, rather than checking finitely many stages.

It also proves the four-prime chain in the boundary polynomial ring under
explicit residue-diagram hypotheses, its pullback through a nonzero-kernel
quotient, the separated principal-prime lemma, and the final dimension bound
under explicit prime-control hypotheses.

**Still missing:** the actual boundary fields and pullback; the plane exhaustion
and pole-budget argument; the density and diagonal choice yielding algebraic
independence; the filtered union, embedding and special fibre; and the
valuation-theoretic prime control in HY §5. In particular,
`dimension_le_two_of_prime_control` assumes the prime-control statement. It does
not prove that statement for the constructed ring. The complete counterexample
therefore remains unverified.

The text uses the construction in the [cited preprint](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.01887v1).
These formalization gaps do not constitute a refutation of that construction
or a detected error in the submitted manuscript.

## Problem 44

The local project states a full `Problem44.Counterexample`: a two-dimensional
complete normal Noetherian local domain, with the required property for every
ideal with maximal radical. The valuation specification requires existence of
a finite DVR representation of the integral closures of all positive powers,
and excludes every such representation with fewer than two DVRs. It cannot be
satisfied merely by displaying a redundant pair or testing finitely many ideals.
No value of this full specification is constructed.

The verified development uses the actual Weierstrass curve and group law. It
proves the discriminant computation and $2Q=(1/4,-7/8)$ for $Q=(0,1)$. It then
proves that $Q$ has infinite order by showing that successive doublings have
strictly decreasing two-adic x-coordinate valuations. This does not assume
Nagell–Lutz or rely on testing a finite list of multiples. Injective base change
and the actual affine coordinate ring's class map establish the corresponding
non-torsion assertions over the complex field and in its ideal class group.

Negative definiteness of the intersection matrix and the algebraic class-group
calculations in the center cases are also verified.

**Still missing:** comparison with line bundles on the complete analytic curve;
plumbing and Grauert contraction; construction and properties of the analytic
local ring and its completion; Rees existence and correspondence; the analytic
blowup comparison; the birational graph, resolution and geometric center
arguments; and Sally's completion transfer. The class-group calculations retain
their explicit geometric and degree hypotheses. They do not establish those
hypotheses or quantify over the primary ideals of a constructed ring.

The completion equivalence used in the text is recorded on printed page 12 of
[Swanson's Rees valuations survey](https://www.math.purdue.edu/~iswanso/reesvals.pdf).
Its formal proof remains outstanding. The distinction between a geometric
construction and explicit algebraic equations remains as described in
[Searching for Cutkosky's example](https://www.math.purdue.edu/~iswanso/DGS.pdf).
